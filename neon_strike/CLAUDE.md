# CLAUDE.md — Neon Strike (Odoo 19 module)

Context for Claude Code. Read this before touching the code.

## What this is

Odoo 19.0 Community module running a space shooter (2D canvas, OWL 2) on a **public `/neon` page** published in the website menu (same as `pingpong_3d`), playable **without a login**: type a nickname and play. It supports **remote co-op for up to 4 players** over the Odoo bus (room by code, host-authoritative) on top of the single-player mode. Each player's identity is a **session token + nickname** (not `res.users`). Single global leaderboard in `neon.strike.score`. No external dependencies beyond `web`, `bus` and `website`.

The run structure is roguelite: **every 5 cleared waves each player keeps 1 of 3 permanent perks** (50 of them) until they die, a **boss every 4 waves** and a **colossal boss every 10** that is wider than the arena and pulls the camera back.

## Architecture

- `static/src/js/game_engine.js` — `NeonStrikeEngine(canvas, {onGameOver, onLocalInput, onAction, role, players, localSlot, names, hotseat})` class. ALL gameplay lives here: rAF loop, physics, enemies (drone/speedy/tank/**sniper**/**kami**/**5 bosses**/**5 colossi**), **asteroids** (`rocks`), power-ups (T/S/B/L), **perks + dash + actives**, **beams**, combos (capped at x25), particles, screen shake, slow-mo, synthesised audio. **N ships** in `this.ships` (array by slot), individual lives, going down (`down`) and reviving by flying next to the others. Simulated in a logical space **shaped like the window** (`_fitArena`, floor 680×540) and constant for the whole match, so coordinates are identical on every machine (the host's size travels in the snapshot). Roles:
  - `solo` — local single-player simulation.
  - `host` — simulates everything; `snapshot()` serialises a compact state to broadcast; applies remote input with `setRemoteInput(slot, tx, ty)`.
  - `guest` — does not simulate; `applySnapshot(snap)` and renders; reports its pointer via `onLocalInput`.
  It imports nothing from Odoo: agnostic and testable.
- `static/src/js/perks.js` — The **50 permanent perks** as data (`PERKS`, `PERK_INDEX`, `MAX_ACTIVES`, `rollOffers`). Each one carries `kind` (passive/conditional/active), `mods` (numbers summed into `ship.mods`) and/or `flags` (behaviour switches the engine branches on), plus `cd` for actives and `req: "coop"` for the two that need a second player. **The array order is the wire format** (the snapshot sends indexes): append at the end, never insert in the middle. Adding a perk = one entry here + the branch in `game_engine.js` if it carries a flag.
- `static/src/js/bosses.js` — The **5 regular bosses** as data (`BOSSES`, `bossForWave`): sprite, tint, collision radius and `hp`/`val` multipliers over the shared boss formula, plus the blurb reused by the glossary. They rotate on the waves that are a multiple of 4; `bossForWave` skips the ones a colossus takes over (multiples of 20) **without losing its place in the rotation**, so all five actually show up. Their AI lives in `_updateBoss` + `_bossWarden/_bossLancer/_bossHive/_bossPrism`, keyed by index.
- `static/src/js/boss_animator.js` — `BossAnimator` + `BOSS_ANIM`: the **animation of the 5 regular bosses**, ported from the "Bullet-hell boss animator" design study. **Render only**, same contract as [ship_flight.js](static/src/js/ship_flight.js). Per boss: DREADNOUGHT leans into its sweep, breathes, runs two rear plumes and throws a burst ring plus a recoiling muzzle flash; WARDEN's armour plates slide out and dim as the shield drops while a gapped curtain rotates and the core pulses through the hurt window; LANCER tilts, stretches into its dive and leaves afterimages; HIVE bobs, chases a light along the rim and flares the bay that actually opened; PRISM collapses to a pinch on every teleport, leaves a shockwave behind and spins a three-armed spiral. **State lives in `engine._bossAnims`, keyed by boss index, never on the enemy** — a guest rebuilds `this.enemies` from every snapshot, which would reset the pose ~15 times a second. `_updateBossAnims` runs from **both** `update()` (after `_updateBeams`, because the LANCER charge glow reads their `warn`) and `_guestUpdate()`. Three deliberate departures from the study: the hulls stay `boss0..boss4` (its own five grids would be an art swap, not an animation), **no lance beam** (the engine owns it, telegraph and hitbox, and it travels as `bm` — a second one would draw light where the damage is not) and **no hit flash or death dissolve** (`e.flash` already does the first and travels as `f`; the second would need the corpse to outlive `killEnemy`, which is gameplay). Reference speeds were **read off the real AI**, not carried over: the study's 90 px/s saturated instantly against a dreadnought that sweeps at 209.
- `static/src/js/colossi.js` — The **5 colossal bosses** as data (`COLOSSI`, `colossusForWave`): sprite, tint, logical width (all wider than the 680 arena), camera `zoom`, hit points, score value and the blurb reused by the glossary. Their AI lives in `_updateColossus` keyed by index, same order. **AEGIS-01 no longer uses `speed`**: it flies `aegis_motion.js`, and `descend.restY` there mirrors its `y`. **VULCAN does not either**: it walks `vulcan_motion.js`, whose `descend.restY` mirrors its `y` the same way. **HYDRA-07's two side heads are separate targets** with hull points of their own (`HYDRA_HEAD` in the engine), so its entry also carries `heads`, `parts` and the director's clock; **VULCAN's two shoulder fans** are the same shape (`VULCAN_FAN`), and its entry carries `parts`, `fans`, the heat and the phase clock. VULCAN's `hp` went 800 -> 1000 to pay for its vent window (the reasoning, and the measurement behind it, is in the comment there).
- `static/src/js/aegis_motion.js` — `AegisMotion` + `AEGIS_MOTION`: the **motion profile of AEGIS-01**, ported from the "AEGIS-01 Study" design study. This is the one thing a design study is allowed to write a position with: it runs on the **host**, inside `_updateColossus`, and the result travels in the snapshot like any other AI. It replaces a constant 30 px/s slide that flipped direction instantly at the ends of a 210 px lane with acceleration-limited travel (the brake is deliberately weaker than the accel, so it overshoots and settles), a pull toward the centre of mass of the **live** ships capped at 46 px and slewed at 9 px/s so it never becomes a chase, a brace that plants the hull for the 0.75 s of the curtain telegraph and lifts it 5 px, one bounded shove during the enrage beat, and a 30 px sag as the hull is chewed down — bounded, because the curtain spawns at `y + 82.8` and falls at 144 px/s, so every px of descent is reaction time taken off the player. Deterministic and `dt`-driven (`mv / 60`), so pause freezes it, slow-mo slows it and an EMP `stun` stops it dead. Measured over a 120 s fight: |v| peaks at 58 px/s, a tick never moves the hull a whole pixel, x stays inside ±140 px of the field centre and y inside 146-180.
- `static/src/js/vulcan_motion.js` — `VulcanMotion` + `VULCAN_MOTION`: the **walk profile of VULCAN**, ported from the "VULCAN Animation Sheet". Second and last of the two design-study files allowed to write a position, on the same terms as `aegis_motion.js`: it runs on the **host**, inside `_updateColossus`, and the result travels in the snapshot. It replaces `e.x += e.vx * mv * 0.55` — a flat 24.75 px/s that flipped direction on the frame it touched either end of a 210 px lane, which is the exact pattern the AEGIS port was written to remove, and least defensible on the one colossus the catalogue calls *a walking foundry*. What it gives instead: acceleration-limited travel with a brake weaker than the accelerator (14 vs 10 px/s², so it overshoots the end of the lane and settles into it), feet that **plant** for the three phases the sheet plants them for (the overheat, the vent and the volley charge — measured: 0 px travelled through all of them, which is most of what makes the vent window read as the machine stopping), a shorter and slower lane once it is limping, and the same bounded sag with hull loss AEGIS has. Deliberately **no pull toward the ships**: AEGIS's blurb promises it leans toward whoever is still flying, VULCAN's promises a machine walking its lane and shooting the arena, and the two must not move alike. Measured over a 70 s fight: |v| ≤ 25 px/s, a tick never moves the hull more than 0.42 px (so the animator never sees a teleport and the gait never skips a stride), x stays inside ±112 px of the field centre.
- `static/src/js/colossus_animator.js` — `ColossusAnimator` + `COLOSSUS_ANIM` + `COLOSSUS_ANIM_KINDS`: the **animation of the colossi**, from the same studies (the Study, then the "AEGIS-01 Animation Sheet" that reworked it) plus the "HYDRA-07 Animation Sheet". **Render only**, same contract as [boss_animator.js](static/src/js/boss_animator.js). **AEGIS**, **HYDRA** and **VULCAN** have a section; `COLOSSUS_ANIM_KINDS` is what decides, and any colossus without one falls through to the plain hull draw. Every effect **promotes** the cell it lands on along the sprite bank's own ramp (`1 9 2 5 3 4 6 7 0`, resolved for the hull's tint through the exported `palette()`) instead of washing additive light over it: a cell is repainted only when the promotion lands on a different rung, which is both why a slab covered in effects still reads as pixel art and why it costs ~160 `fillRect`s in an average frame (425 at the worst) instead of the 2576 of a full repaint. Only the enrage charge and its ring are still additive. It **tips toward whoever is still flying** (the catalogue blurb promises it, and drift alone could not deliver it: AEGIS is stationary 95% of the fight, so the lean takes the centre of mass of the live ships as its main term and the drift as the second), breathes harder as it takes damage, **braces on a telegraph** (squares up, judders, dims its nozzles and saturates the core), recoils on the salvo and **flashes the two siege nozzles the salvo actually leaves from**, lights the bottom edge cell by cell as the curtain leaves it, hangs plumes off the four nozzle clusters phase-shifted inner against outer, pulses a **core** that grows a ring of light from the middle, slides toward the lean and goes cold as the hull fails, burns out 30% of its dark cells and vents sparks under 30% hull, and — the one that matters — **opens a shutter over the hole in the next curtain**, because `gap` is decided one curtain ahead and travels as `gp`. Nothing about the hull is written down per colossus: the nozzles are the columns that reach below the bottom edge and the core is the glass, both read out of the art in `hullGeometry`. State lives in `engine._colossusAnims`, keyed by colossus index, never on the enemy; `_updateColossusAnims` runs from **both** `update()` and `_guestUpdate()` and feeds it `_liveCentroidX()`, and `_colossusCue` mirrors the one-shots (`curtain`, `salvo`, `rage`) as `cfx` events. Five departures from the studies: the hull stays `colossus0` from the sprite bank, **no lance** (the engine has no such attack and a beam with no hitbox shows light where the damage is not), **no hit flash** (a colossus is under fire every frame), the **enrage beat is an envelope started by the `rage` cue** because `e.hold` does not travel, and **no state machine and no DEATH** — the sheet drives itself through ENTRY/IDLE/LEAN/PLANT/CURTAIN/SALVO/STAGGER/DEATH and fires its own bullets from it, while here every one of those beats is observed from what the engine already owns, and a death sequence would need the corpse to outlive `killEnemy`. **Every number was re-measured against this arena**, which is what the port is actually made of: the study's core breath (0.18..0.50) never once lit a pixel, because glass sits one rung under white and nothing below k = 0.5 changes a cell at all.
  **HYDRA-07** is the second section, and unlike AEGIS the port went the other way round: the sheet described a boss the engine did not have, so **the engine moved to meet it**. `_hydra` in `game_engine.js` is now a director — under `COLOSSUS_RAGE_AT` the spiral and the fans **take turns** with a breath between them (each one gets read on its own), over it they run at once, which is the whole second phase for no new pattern. The spiral gained a 45-frame wind-up that telegraphs **which way it will turn** (the crown flips `spin` when the beat opens and turns continuously, so the direction is always readable), and the two fans now fire `HYDRA_FAN.stagger` frames apart instead of in the same frame, because being able to read which cone lands first is the point of having two. The **side heads are destructible**: `HYDRA_HEAD` gives each 12% of the hull's points, `_headAt` gives it a box of its own out of `hullParts`, and `_damageEnemy` takes an impact position and routes the hit there. Head damage never touches the hull — killing one has to be something you fly out to the flank and *choose*, or the trade it buys is not a trade. What it buys: that head's fan leaves the fight and the crown's spiral tightens by `deadStep`, until it grows back 15.7 s later. Kill both and HYDRA stops aiming at you entirely and becomes a wall of spiral.
  The animation of all that is built on the fact that `colossus1` **already is** the sheet's monster: a crown with two glass eyes over a chest with four neon grilles, and a head with its own glass at the end of each arm. So `hullGeometry` grew a second half that reads *parts* out of the art the way it already read nozzles — the crown is whatever sits above the row where the silhouette first spans 40% of its width, the heads are the outer two of the three pieces it breaks into below its widest row (AEGIS breaks into four nozzle clusters and therefore has none) cut back to what sits within `HEAD_SPAN` of the mouth, the vents are the cells already painted in the neon accent — and `hullParts` exports those as fractions of the hull, which is the **one thing the animator exports that the simulation reads**: the spiral leaves the lens between the crown's eyes, each fan leaves its own head's glass, and the box you shoot is the cells that light up, all from one answer to "where is that part". On top of them: a **lens** on the same machinery as AEGIS's core window; a **ring of light on the crown's plating** that burns in as many arcs as there are arms while emitting and runs a single arc *the way the coming spiral turns* while it winds up; two **side heads** that blink on beats of their own (0.31 and 0.35 Hz, half a period apart), open their mouths on the telegraph, slide that light towards the ships and flash a muzzle flame a beat apart; the four **chest grilles** rippling downwards once per turn of the crown; and the local **hit flash**, the **dead head** pulled down the ramp with sparks at its stump, and the **rebuild** growing back out of that stump behind a lit front with the eyes last. Those last three cost no cue at all — a head's points travel, so a drop is a hit, zero is a destroyed head and the countdown under it is the rebuild. The enrage is a level read off `hp01` against `COLOSSUS_RAGE_AT` rather than AEGIS's beat, and the hull rears up 9 px over the 50 frames `_bossRage` holds fire. Four numbers are shared with the engine rather than copied — `HYDRA_SPIRAL` owns the arm angle and count, and `sa`/`sp`/`hd` travel — so the lit arc cannot end up anywhere except on the cells the last pair of bullets left from. Measured over a 90 s fight it costs one `drawImage` plus ~122 `fillRect`s in an average frame (325 at the worst), and every idle level was set against the ramp until it went quiet: the mouths repaint nothing at all on 41% of idle frames, which is what makes the blink a blink.

  **VULCAN** is the third section, and it went the HYDRA way: the sheet described a **heat cycle** the engine did not have, so the engine moved. `_vulcan` in `game_engine.js` is a director over one loop — `REST → BEAM_WARN → BEAM → OVERHEAT → VENT → ROCK_WARN → ROCKS` — where **heat is the clock**: it climbs at 1/210 per frame while the forge beams sweep and the phase ends when the gauge fills, not when a timer does. Measured: one cycle is 600 frames (10.0 s), the vent is 22% of the fight, and the telegraph is up 13% of it (`ring` on the overheat, `volley` on the charge). Three things make it a fight rather than a sequence. **The player owns the clock**: damage spent on a shoulder fan buys heat in proportion (`VULCAN_FAN.heatFull`, per *damage* and not per hit, so the lever reads the same on any build), and the overheat **kills the beams where they are**. That is a real trade and it was measured to be one — putting 16% of a constant DPS into the fans costs 9% of your damage and takes **71% of the time under live beams** away (223 s → 64 s over a 300 s bench), while raising the bullet count, so it buys safety by changing the *kind* of pressure rather than the amount. **Breaking a fan is the overshoot penalty**: it seizes for 12 s, so it takes no more heat, the vent window it would have fed gets 30% shorter, and — the part that matters — with no working exhaust the heat comes out of the slot instead, a full circle from the middle of the hull, which is exactly where the core window has spent the whole fight inviting the player to stand. Without that backfire, jamming both fans was the *safe* play and the penalty rewarded the mistake. The fans also **repair** at 1/480 of their points per frame: without it the lever is spent after seven hits and the rest of the run goes back to VULCAN's own clock. **The vent window is the game's only damage multiplier** (`VULCAN.vent.coreMul`, x2) and it is deliberately a *place* rather than a state — the box comes from `hullParts`, so what is worth double is exactly the white middle the hull shows is open, while the rings leave from the shoulders and the safe pocket between them is that same box. `_damageEnemy` routes a hit to a fan before the hull and never takes fan damage off it, same rule as HYDRA's heads. Two corrections to what the art was already saying: the volley leaves from the slot (and its telegraph carries a **number** — one pip per rock, `vn` travels for that reason) fanned **down and outwards**, because the engine's asteroids have no gravity and anything lobbed upwards would climb out of the arena and never be culled; and the beams now leave from `parts.arms` — the hand at the outer-bottom corner of each side arm, ±0.482 of the width, right where the silhouette ends — instead of a fraction of the width nothing draws. **The beams themselves got the sheet's look**: `_drawForgeBeam` draws each one as four filled ribbons whose edges follow a wave travelling down the beam (an outer sheath in the boss tint, a hotter inner one, a white core that jitters a pixel or two along its length) plus a bloom where it crosses the field. It is a per-beam `hot` flag rather than a restyle of `_drawBeams`, because NYX and OMEGA were not part of this pass; `hot` travels as the 8th slot of `bm`. Ribbons rather than a run of variable-width strokes for a measured reason: the same picture costs **12 rasterising calls a frame instead of 212**, and the edges come out continuous instead of stepped every 46 px. Worth being clear about why this was missed first time round — "no lance" (the rule that keeps AEGIS and LANCER beamless) is about *adding* light where there is no hitbox, and says nothing against drawing a beam the engine already owns properly.
  The animation is built on the fact that `colossus2` **already is** the sheet's machine: six chimneys in three heights, a slot painted as a dark frame around a neon ring around a white middle, one accent blob per shoulder, two feet. So `hullGeometry` grew the mirror of its own nozzle rule — a chimney is a *narrow* stack above the first row wide enough to be the hull, a fan is an accent *blob* the slot is not made of — and both tests were checked against all five hulls so that only `colossus2` answers to either. The heat reads off the hull because those three slot layers have three different amounts of ramp headroom: the frame has six rungs and is the gauge (capped under its fourth, which is the bank's fixed grey-blue), the ring has two and carries the top of it by making the white middle *grow* from 18 cells wide to 28, and the middle has none — it is already at the top — so it takes no part. Using it as the cold end was tried and is exactly the trough mistake: 106 cells repainted on every idle frame, and VULCAN never once looking like its own sprite. **The art is the baseline; heat only ever adds.** On top: the pods breathe out of phase with a bright cell orbiting inside each (the only way a cached raster can spin a blade) at a rate *and* a brightness that both read the heat, a hit flash on the fan alone, a jam pulled back down the ramp with sparks at its rim and a clearing that grows back behind a lit front; smoke off the six stacks as **particles** — emitted from `observe` off the animator's own LCG so the draw never consumes simulation noise, rising and slowing without falling back, drifting with the walk and fading over ~2.4 s, with each stack on its own beat and going out one at a time as the hull fails (which is where the sheet's DEATH state ends up in a port that has none). A fixed-height plume came first and was wrong twice over: it reads as a bar chart of the heat rather than as exhaust, and at 35 cells at rest against the puffs' 8 it was also the more expensive. The hands at the ends of the two arms light under the sight line and burn while the beam is live; cracks through the plating on the overheat, taken from the *end* of the same dark-cell order `_drawDamage` burns from the front; and a walk that is the hull rather than four legs — a bob and a settle quantised to whole cells, the foot that just landed lighting up and throwing dust, and a limp under the rage threshold. The gait runs off **distance travelled, not a clock**, which is what makes it ease into every reversal and stop dead while the feet are planted for free — and it is also why gating it on `landed` was a real host/guest split, since a guest derives that from a velocity it only ever sees at ~15 Hz. Measured over a 60 s fight it costs one `drawImage` plus ~218 `fillRect`s in an average frame (366 at the worst, against 1992 for a full repaint), plus 12 rasterising calls for the two beams while they are up; the slot repaints **nothing at all** at rest, and 14% of the cold frames repaint no hull cell whatsoever — lower than HYDRA's 41% because VULCAN's two fans breathe out of phase on purpose, so their troughs rarely coincide.

- `static/src/js/drone_animator.js` — `DRONE_ANIM` + `dronePose` + `drawDrone` + `drawDroneWreck` + `droneTier`: the **animation of the drones**, ported from the "DRONE-A Animation Kit" and "DRONE-B" design studies. **Render only**, same contract as [boss_animator.js](static/src/js/boss_animator.js), but built the opposite way round because a drone is *filler*: there are dozens on screen and every cost is multiplied by the instance count. So there is **no animator object and no state machine per drone** — one shared timeline, sampled with a per-instance phase offset, and the engine already provides it: the zigzag is `sin(e.t * DRONE_ANIM.drift.rate)` and `e.t` is seeded at random per hull, so `e.t` *is* the phase. **Nothing new travels**: `tt`, `h` and `f` were already in the snapshot, which is why a guest draws the same pose. Four pieces: the chassis **leans** into the drift over 5 steps with the eyes leading it by a cell (baked, not sheared at draw time — a matrix shear of a 16 px hull lands on half pixels); the outer **lamps light** on the side it is about to turn towards, 22 frames before the reversal, blinking at 6 Hz (one `fillRect`); the **hull points are the sprite**, with the bright core growing outwards as the tier rises; and the **death** is the one with frames, because it is seen hundreds of times a run — five frames of white silhouette, the eyes out, then the chassis splits on its own symmetry axis and the halves leave with the drift it was carrying, plus four fragments (2 clipped `drawImage` + 4 `fillRect`, no particle system). Per living hull it is **1 `drawImage` and at most 2 `fillRect`**, out of an atlas of ~45 tiny canvases per chassis baked on first use. Two deliberate departures from the study: the hulls stay `drone0`/`drone1` (its own grid would be an art swap), and the hull tiers were **inverted** — the study eroded the art *down* from tier 4, which would have left every drone of waves 1-8 a dark husk since they are all tier 1 until hull points start growing on wave 9. The art is the baseline and light is only ever added, so brightness drains as a hull is worn down. `HIT` needed no code at all: `e.flash` already paints the silhouette and `e.hp` drops on the same frame, so the hull that comes out of the flash is already the lower tier. Colours are **promoted** along the bank's ramp like the colossus animator does, so a lit lamp is the glass the drone's own eyes are painted in and a tier-4 core is its own tint lighter — measured: 0/16/32/52 cells of 72 change between tiers, and no tone outside the art's own palette is ever painted. **DRONE-B** (`drone1`) is the same kit on a *radial* chassis, and which kit a hull gets is read off the art rather than off its name: a chassis that paints the neon accent in **all four quadrants** is radial (those cells are its arms), which `drone0` fails and `drone1` passes, so a third chassis would answer the question on its own. Three pieces change. A cross cannot lean, so it **turns**: `angle(t) = t * spin.rate - cos(t * drift.rate) * spin.swing`, whose derivative carries the drone's own lateral velocity — the study modulates the spin by "the direction of the zigzag", and on this engine the sine *is* the velocity, since `game_engine.js` adds it to x every frame. It therefore runs at 0.05 rad/frame through the middle of a sweep and stalls at 0.01 at the reversal, which parks the stall on the turn tell for free. Only the **arms and the eyes** actually turn (the body is a diamond, and rotating a 16 px diamond only costs it its pixels), quantised to **8 baked steps**; an arm is turned by **moving its two ends and drawing the line back in**, because rotating it cell by cell rounds three neighbours onto a ragged and sometimes broken run — the hull is 16x13, so its centre sits on a half cell in y and even the 90° step lands off the lattice. On step 0 the line lays the cells back exactly where the sprite painted them, so the art stays the baseline here too. The **turn tell** becomes the tips of the two arms on the side it is turning towards, which is a different pair of cells at every step (the arms sit at ±39°, so no step ever leaves one on the axis: it is always two per side). The **death** is radial — the four arms let go first and keep tumbling at the rate the chassis was turning at, and the core, baked with the arms already off it, folds last. **Nothing new travels** for any of it: `v` was already the chassis in the snapshot and the pose is still a pure function of `tt`. Cost is 1 `drawImage` + 0.33 `fillRect` per hull, out of 32 canvases per chassis and tint (4 tiers x 8 steps, 20x21 cells each — a turning arm leaves the grid on both axes, so the frame is padded further than DRONE-A's). The port was verified the way the contract asks: replaying the same workload against the old and new modules left **DRONE-A byte-identical over 33,201 canvas ops**.
- `static/src/js/fry_animator.js` — `FRY_ANIM` + `drawFry` + `drawFryWreck` + `fryKit`/`fryStep`/`fryTier`/`fryDeathFrames` + `fryCard`: the **animation of the four small ships** (speedy, tank, sniper, kamikaze), ported from the "FRY Study" design sheet. Built like the drone kit and for the same reason — **no animator object and no per-instance state**, one shared timeline sampled from each hull's own `e.t` — and shaped end to end by the sheet's one idea: *these are ships, they move by burning fuel, not by moving their parts*. So the hull is **rigid** (nothing on it ever moves relative to anything else), it **turns as a body**, and at rest the only thing that animates is **the burn**: a two-`fillRect` plume on the thrust axis plus a one-cell integer translate of the `drawImage` along that axis on the burn frame. What separates the four is what their engines are doing. SPEEDY runs one hard burn and points where it is *going*; TANK holds a mass down on two heavy engines at 3 Hz and **throttles down to steady the shot** while yawing at whoever it picked; SNIPER runs no main engine at all because it is not travelling but *holding a position* — one side thruster puffs, then the other, and the tell is that **the thrusters cut** and it goes dead still, unpowered, before the sight line arrives; KAMIKAZE has no idle, it is under full thrust from spawn and its burn *is* its speedometer. Every death opens with the same **flame-out** — the plume is gone on the first frame and gutters as a single cell twice — so the hull is falling unpowered before it breaks.
  **Everything geometric is read out of the art** (`geometryOf`), which is what makes it the module's hulls and not the study's six grids, and the art answered every question the sheet asks. The tail is the top edge and its outline is the darkest rung the hull uses, so the *nozzle band* is whatever the first tail row paints in anything else: `tank0` paints two four-cell openings either side of a dark spine and gets two nozzles at ±3 cells, `speedy1` paints one and gets one. A run of **dark tint** on that row is a *recessed* exhaust, which is the whole chassis test — `speedy0` sinks its nozzle and burns focused, bright and stuttering at 10 Hz, `speedy1`'s tail is a flush plate so its burn is broad, soft and half the rhythm. `sniper0`'s station-keeping thrusters are the accent lamps already painted on its lower corners, its cannon is the run of rows the silhouette narrows to (the mirror of the colossus nozzle rule), and `kami0`'s core is its block of hot white — promoted on the *ring around it*, because the core itself is already at the top of the ramp and has no headroom (the lesson VULCAN's gauge was rebuilt on).
  **Wear** is the hull-points read and it goes the honest way round: tier 0 is the art untouched, and damage demotes a hashed subset of the plating (never the accent, the glass or the hot core — the neon layer is what an effect lights, not what plating does). One ramp per type, because a tier means a different thing to each: a TANK at wave 1 has mhp 4 so its tiers are exactly hp 4/3/2/1, while a SPEEDY is mhp 1 until wave 10 and at mhp 2 lands on **tier 0 and tier 2** (0.5 is not greater than 0.50), which is the wear state it is actually seen in for twenty waves. Measured off the real grids: 12-14% of the plating repaints at tier 1, 25-43% at tier 2 and 43-49% at tier 3, with no dead step anywhere — which is the point of the **ladder**. An effect walks a cell along the rungs *this hull is painted with* (`geo.ladder`), not the bank's nine; the two older animators fold an unused rung onto the nearest used one *after* the fact, which silently turns a step into a no-op (`speedy1` uses no metal, so demoting its tint by one rung folded straight back onto the tint and the whole first tier of wear repainted nothing).
  Four departures from the sheet, all forced by measurement. **The yaw is the flight path**: the study steers a SPEEDY off `clamp((shipX - x) / 40)`, which against a 680 px arena is pinned at the stop essentially always, so the hull points along the velocity the AI is actually applying (`FRY_ANIM.speedy.steer`/`fall`, read from here by `_updateEnemies`) — measured at wave 1 that spreads over all five steps, 6/29/20/36/9%. **The kamikaze is turned live, not baked**: it is the one hull that turns through the whole circle, and a 14x14 diamond rotated cell by cell only loses its pixels (the DRONE-B lesson), so it keeps the matrix rotation `drawSprite` already gave it and only its core throb is baked — which also took the atlas from the study's 1.8 MB to **742 KB over all six hulls, every tier and every pose**. **The sniper does not bank either**: its drift is a whole-cell translate, which is both what station keeping is and the only thing a hull with two-cell eyes survives being asked to do three quarters of the time. And **the study's TANK B barrel has no art** — neither `tank0` nor `tank1` has one — so both take the hull-yaw tell, and the muzzle bead went with it (the engine already draws the aimed sight line for `telK === "aimed"`); what is left is the part with art behind it, the recoil kick and a flash on the hull edge the shot actually leaves from.
  Cost, measured over 5,400 hull draws across every hull, tier and pose: **1 `drawImage` + 1.92 `fillRect`, never more than 2**. The plume owns both cells and every tell is built by *reallocating* them — TANK throttles to one ember and spends the other on the recoil flash, SNIPER's charge bead takes the cell its second puff was using, the hit flash spends none at all. **Nothing new travels except the recoil** (`fi`, on the wire only for the three frames it lasts): the kamikaze's throttle is rebuilt from `tt` rather than from a velocity a guest never sees, and everything else was already in the snapshot. `e.t` is now seeded at random for speedy and sniper the way tank's and drone's already were — it is the only phase the kit has, and two snipers side by side were puffing on the same frame and cutting together.
- `static/src/js/ship_flight.js` also exports **`shipCard`**, the glossary's live card for a player hull, on the same `{ width, height, draw(g, t) }` contract as the enemy ones. It is the only card whose animation has **state** (`ShipFlight` integrates), so it owns its own instance and steps it with the elapsed time, and the probe pass that measures the canvas uses a second one. Its flight path is `CARD_PATH`, and it is worth reading the comment there before retuning it: the obvious plain sine weave, peaking at 672 px/s against a `rollSpeed` of 520, rolled **zero** times in 20 seconds. The `swing` term is bounded by the damping at ~0.20 against a `rollTrigger` of 0.26 so it is very nearly unreachable, which makes the *flip* the trigger that matters — and a flip only counts while the hull is still fast, i.e. only on a weave quick enough to leave the damped bank behind. A weave fast enough to roll at all rolls *continuously*, which is worse, so the path is amplitude-modulated: gentle most of the time, one hard flick when the envelope spikes. Measured: 6 rolls per 30 s, rolling 8% of the time, all five tilt frames at 11/25/28/25/11%. `ay` is deliberately small (55, not 90) for a reason that is not about flight — the flame grows with thrust and the canvas is cut to what the loop paints, so a hull that pushes hard makes a taller card and therefore a *smaller* hull once the 118 px art box has scaled it.
- `static/src/js/backgrounds.js` — The **27 places** a run flies through (`BACKGROUNDS`, `backgroundForWave`, `Backdrop`): deep space, nebulae, a black hole, a wormhole, a supernova, a pulsar, planet flybys, six planet surfaces (gas/ice/lava/ocean/jungle/desert/storm), a ship graveyard, an orbital station, an asteroid belt… One per wave, **in order, cycling**. `backgroundForWave` is pure, which is what lets host and guests paint the same sky with **zero bytes on the bus**; the backdrop is never simulated and never enters the snapshot. Each entry names a `kind` (the painter) plus params, so 27 places come out of 22 painters. A painter may implement `init` (state), `paint` (static art, **baked once** into an offscreen canvas at half resolution), `update` + `live` (per-frame). Only put in `live` what genuinely moves: it runs at 60 fps behind the whole game. Painters draw in **logical arena coordinates**, over the box the camera reaches when it pulls back for a colossus.
  **Places 1-5 are pixel art** (the PLACES Study, Direction A, ported 2026-08-29): `deep`, `planet_blue`, `nebula_violet`, `belt` and `blackhole` bake from two more phases instead of `paint` -- `field(bd, x, y)` returns the place as a scalar 0..1, sampled once per 3 px art pixel and snapped to an 8-rung ramp through a Bayer 4x4 dither, and `hard(bd, g, pix)` lays hard-edged art over it -- so the sky lands on the same grid as the hulls and the seam at the play field is gone. Their ramps live in the entry's `p` (`ramp`, `landRamp`, `starRamp`, `topRung`), which is what keeps the data file the place to retune them, and everything they draw live snaps to the same lattice. Places 6-27 are still soft gradient art and still use `paint`.
  **GAS GIANT DESCENT (place 6) is the "Gas Giant Descent" study's smooth branch** (version B; the study prefers its pixel version A, but A is the treatment places 1-5 already have and B keeps place 6 in the language of the twenty-one it sits among). The whole study lands on the shared `surface` painter as parameters, so the other six atmosphere worlds run the same code path — verified rather than asserted: **26 of the 27 places hash byte-identical over the full 1428x1162 box and their 272 px glossary thumbnail**, before and after. New axes: `flow` (−1 = the deck rises past you, which is the whole fix for "the name says DESCENT and nothing descends"), `bandForm` ("belt" vs today's "bank"), `bandSpread`, `skyStops`, `glow`, `decks[]`, `flakes`, `vortices` and `density`; identity on all of them is today's bank world. A belt world is four tiles that wrap vertically and translate at their own rate (0.16 / 0.30 / 0.42 / 1.10 px a frame, each step ~2.6x), because **shear is only visible as a rate difference** and one plane can never paint it. Everything is baked in `paint`; `live` is `drawImage` only. Its veil is **0** — measured, not chosen: at frame 1500 the arena reads mean L 0.0397 and p95 0.0854 on a 3 px lattice in linear light, against a ceiling of 0.098 for 4:1 contrast with the brightest enemy bullet, so it is the second place in the catalogue that needs no curtain at all (and a place carrying its own `p.veil` is now composed at full value rather than the 0.85 the soft places are dimmed to). The near plane's density ramp is **strips of constant alpha** rather than the study's scratch plus `destination-in`: the same picture to a maximum channel difference of 2 over the whole box, for one box area of fill instead of three — 8.5 ms a frame down to 4.6 at zoom 1 on a CPU rasteriser. This place is **blit-bound by construction**, which is the cheap side of the trade on a GPU-backed canvas and the expensive one without, so count box areas before adding a plane. The engine's near star field now takes the place's `flow` (`bgFlow`), which the study left open and recommended: without it the backdrop rises while the stars fall, and you can see it. The study's own departures are listed in the file header, and the two that matter are that a **point light does not come off the field ramp** (EVENT HORIZON's is entirely warm, so its stars were 3 px amber squares on black -- exactly a bullet) and that the black hole's dust is now a **Keplerian inspiral** riding the squashed disc rather than the old screen-plane Newtonian integrator, which could not sit *in* a disc drawn at 0.42 squash and used `Math.random()` this file forbids. Each entry also carries the **`desc` the glossary prints**, so the catalogue of places is one list and not two. `BG_SCRIM` and `bgScrim(def)` live here as well (the engine reads the result precomputed as `bd.scrim`; the export is the retuning surface), and `backdropThumb(def, w)` paints the **glossary still**: the frame is composed at arena size and scaled down — painting it small would shrink the sky and not the things in it — with the live painters stepped forward `THUMB_WARMUP` frames first, or half of them would come out empty (comet still off screen, rings still inside the remnant, beams not yet turned), then the scrim and the star field on top, so the card shows what you actually fly in.
- `static/src/js/sprites.js` — Pixel art sprite bank. Each sprite is a character grid (1 char = 1 logical pixel); symmetric ones are written at half width and mirrored (`mir: true`). Indices `4/5/6` are **tint**: re-coloured at draw time with the ship/enemy colour, so one hull serves the 4 slots and the enemy variants. API: `sprite(name, tint, px, flash)` (rasterized canvas, cached by name+colour+scale+flash), `drawSprite(g, name, x, y, {tint, px, flash, rot, alpha})`, `spriteSize(name)`, `spriteGrid(name)` (the expanded character grid, mirroring resolved — shared and cached on the definition, so **read only**; `colossus_animator.js` uses it to know which cells an effect may touch), `pxFor(name, target)`, `bankSprite(name, tint, px, level)`, `rgba(hex, a)` (hex at an alpha as a canvas fill — the one copy; the engine's `glow` and the menu backdrop's local `rgba` were folded into it, `backgrounds.js` keeps its own only because it deliberately imports nothing), `palette(tint)` (the index -> colour map the rasterizer itself uses, exported so `colossus_animator.js` promotes cells to exactly the colours the raster was painted with instead of keeping a second copy of them), `RAMP_CHARS`/`RUNG`/`TINT_RUNGS` (the same palette read as a brightness ramp, `1 9 2 5 3 4 6 7 0`, which is a property of the bank and not of any one animator: all three animators read it), `rungFold(used)` (the one copy of the fold that keeps a promotion on a colour the hull is actually painted with — it was the same fifteen lines in `colossus_animator.js` and `drone_animator.js`, and a third copy in `fry_animator.js` would have been the point where they drift) and `canvasBounds(cv, pad)` (the box the painted pixels of a canvas occupy, which is how the glossary's animated cards size themselves). **The tilt frames are not image files**: `bankSprite` builds them from the hull's own pixel grid, one logical pixel column at a time (columns compressed towards the centre, each scaled vertically by how near that side now is), which is what keeps the sprite bank the single source of hull art and keeps the per-slot tint working — 20 PNGs could do neither. Levels are -2..2, level 0 returns the flat sprite, and the result is cached like `sprite()`. Imports nothing: usable outside Odoo. Sprites: `ship0..3`, `drone0/1`, `speedy0/1`, `tank0/1`, `sniper0`, `kami0`, `boss0`, `colossus0..4` (84-100 px grids, drawn 780-1000 logical px wide), `pupT/pupS/pupB/pupL`, `rock0/1`. Power-ups carry the glyph (T/S/bomb/+) drawn in the grid itself, so the sprite name is `"pup" + p.t` and the tint comes from `PUP_COLORS`: adding a power-up type means touching both places.
- `static/src/js/glossary.js` — `GLOSSARY`: data-only catalogue (groups → cards with `sprite`, `tint`, `px`, `label`, `sub`, `desc`) feeding the "Ships, enemies and powers" panel in the menu. The **colossal boss group is generated from `colossi.js`**, the **places group from `backgrounds.js`** (those cards carry a `bg` descriptor instead of a `sprite`, and their art is a painted still from `backdropThumb`) and the **twelve hull cards carry a `kit`** — the four player ships and the eight small fry — which makes them *live canvases* instead of pictures: `shipCard`/`droneCard`/`fryCard` each hand the component a canvas size and one function that paints a frame, `CARD_KITS` says which painter a kit gets, and `_startCards` in `neon_strike_game.js` drives all twelve off a single rAF for as long as the panel is open (`prefers-reduced-motion` gets one painted frame, same contract as the menu backdrop). A hull whose entire language is its engine says nothing in a still, so each card runs the thing its `desc` line describes — SPEEDY leaning through its whole arc while the burn stutters, TANK playing the engine's real 150-frame cycle telegraph and shot included, SNIPER charging on its real 70-frame clock, KAMIKAZE throbbing at the cap, and the player hulls banking through all five tilt frames with the flame swelling, the retros firing and a barrel roll every few seconds. **The canvas size is measured, not chosen**: every frame of the loop is stacked onto one probe and `canvasBounds` takes the union, so the frame fits whatever the art does and nothing jitters as it runs. Bosses, colossi, ships, asteroids and capsules are still flat sprites, and the perk section is built in the OWL component (`perkGroups`) straight from `PERKS`, so none of them can drift from the engine. The tints are the engine's and the `desc` lines describe the **real AI** in `game_engine.js`: if you change behaviour (HP, points, firing pattern), review this file. It covers all 19 sprites in the bank. An item that carries a **`practice` descriptor** (`{type, v}`, `{boss: k}`, `{colossus: k}` or `{rock: v}`) can be fought on its own from the picker — this file is the **only** place that roster exists, which is why the backend menu is a bare URL and nothing about it has to be repeated in Python.
- `static/src/js/menu_backdrop.js` — `MenuBackdrop(canvas)` class (`start()`/`destroy()`): animated backdrop for the start menu in "attract mode". Parallax star field + drifting enemies/asteroids + the 4 ships firing, all through `sprites.js`. It is **decorative**: it does not simulate, make sound, read input or touch the bus; it deliberately does not reuse `NeonStrikeEngine` (that would drag in audio, input and game over). It scales sprites with `zoom = clamp(width/680, 1, 1.6)` because the menu is wider than the game's logical space. Honours `prefers-reduced-motion` by painting a single frame.
- `static/src/js/hud.js` — the **heads-up display**, ported from the "HUD Study" design study. Everything the HUD draws is bitmap pixels on the hulls' own grid: a 3x5 font baked into one atlas per colour (a glyph is one `drawImage`) and shapes built from whole `fillRect`s. No `fillText`, no `arc`, no gradient, no stroke — which is why the call count roughly triples while the frame cost drops. The argument: **the play field claims the window**. Lives, dash and bombs move into one band on the bottom edge, split around a 72 px void the ship flies through so all three sit in the same peripheral field as the hitbox dot; the three are told apart by *silhouette* (chevron, upright bar, diamond) because peripherally they are only shapes, and quantity is read as length rather than counted. Everything else moves outward in priority order — crew tags bottom-left, actives bottom-right, capsules up the right edge, combo top-centre, score/wave/clock/graze in the furthest corner — or off the play screen entirely (the 16 perk dots went to the Esc overlay). Every element is an **anchor plus an offset in logical px**, never a fraction of W/H, because the arena is shaped from the window. `HudFx` is the transitions, and it is **render-only in the same sense as `ShipFlight`**: it *observes* lives, bombs and dash charges and starts an envelope wherever one moved, so a guest gets them off the snapshot for free. One new colour, `#ff4d5e`, reserved for one meaning (last life / down). Two numbers the study needed that the wire did not have are now in `snapshot()`: `dp` (how full the recharging dash pip is, 0-100 — the *fill* and not the clock, because the length comes off perks the HUD would otherwise have to be told about) and `bt` (frames left per capsule, so the column drains and the last two seconds flicker; `bf` stays, it is wire format). Entry points are still `_renderHud` / `_renderPerkHud` in `game_engine.js`.
- `static/src/js/perk_screen.js` — `PerkScreen`: the **between-waves upgrade screen**, ported from the "Upgrade Study". Render-only, on the same terms as `HudFx` and `ShipFlight`: it holds the card animation and nothing the simulation reads, it is not in the snapshot, and it advances from the `ts` `_updatePerkPhase` hands it, so pause freezes it. It replaces three 188x250 cards at a fixed centre and a line of prose with the thing the player is actually asking — *what does this do to the ship I already have?* Three parts: **modifier rows** carrying the perk's delta next to what the ship's number becomes (`FIRE DELAY +15% → 8.5f`) with a 2x8 stub against any modifier the ship already carries, so stacking is visible rather than remembered; the **build block** (families with counts, active slots, current totals) as the context those deltas are read against, a left rail at W ≥ 880 and a strip under the title below it; and a **measured layout** — card height comes from `measureText` at the live card width, the three are levelled to the tallest, and `_perkCards()` returns the same rect array `draw` paints from, so a card can never be clickable somewhere it is not drawn. **`MOD_BASES` lives in `game_engine.js`**, next to `_fireDelay`'s base, `SHIP_HIT_R` and `DASH_CD`, and travels in the model: the running totals must not drift from the code that applies them. Five departures, all in the file header, and three of them are the same shape — *the study describes a state this engine cannot reach*: the **4/4-actives blocked card** (`rollOffers` already drops actives from the pool at the cap, so an offer never contains one), the **dead-player lockout** (`_updatePerkPhase` waits on downed ships and `pickPerk` grants to them, so a dead player still chooses) and the study's timeout fix that follows from the first. The other two are that **Silkscreen and IBM Plex Mono are not shipped** (structure is `system-ui` like the rest of the HUD, everything numeric is monospace so the columns line up) and that **35 of the 50 perks carry no `mods` at all**, so a card with no modifier rows always keeps its description — the study's pool was all numbers and ours is two thirds flags. The `RAIL_W` threshold is 880 and not the study's 1100, measured against what `_fitArena` can actually build (1134x540 at the aspect clamp).
- `static/src/js/neon_strike_game.js` — OWL component `NeonStrikeGame` (template `neon_strike.Game`). Screens `state.screen` = `menu | lobby | game`. Uses `rpc` (to `/neon/*`) and `bus_service` (NOT `orm`: `call_kw` is `auth="user"`). Creates/destroys the engine with `useEffect` when entering/leaving `game`. The host broadcasts a snapshot ~15 Hz (`/neon/state`); the guest forwards its pointer ~20 Hz (`/neon/input`). Subscribes to `ns_lobby|ns_start|ns_state|ns_input|ns_end`. It is **not** a client action: at the end of the file a `whenReady` mounts the component as a standalone OWL app (`makeEnv`+`startServices`+`mount` with `getTemplate`) on `.o_neon_strike_root` if present.
- `controllers/main.py` — `NeonStrikeController` (`http.Controller`). `GET /neon` (`auth="public"`, `website=True`, `sitemap=True`) renders `neon_strike.page` and makes sure the session token exists. JSON routes `auth="public"` (`/neon/create|join|start|input|state|score|solo_score|leave|scores`) operating with `sudo()` and resolving the player from `request.session["neon_token"]`.
- `static/src/xml/neon_strike_templates.xml` — OWL templates: `neon_strike.Game` (menu with **nickname input** / lobby / game), `neon_strike.Pause`, `neon_strike.Glossary`, `neon_strike.Feedback` and `neon_strike.Leaderboard`. `t-out` (not `t-esc`). The play screen is the arena plus a 20 px page rail carrying the Odoo attribution — below the canvas, so it never covers the field and never scrolls away. Resuming closes the glossary, because a running game under an open panel is the one state the overlay stack must not reach.
- `views/neon_strike_page.xml` — QWeb `neon_strike.page`: uses `t-call="web.frontend_layout"` (only depends on `web`; brings frontend assets + session info) and contains `.o_neon_strike_root` (positioned full screen by CSS). Adds `generator`/`author` = Odoo meta via `additional_head` and removes the chrome with `no_header`/`no_footer`.
- `static/src/scss/neon_strike.scss` — Styles. `body.o_neon_strike_page`/`.o_neon_strike_root` full screen. Odoo purple `#714B67` (brand assets: https://www.odoo.com/page/brand-assets) in the toolbar/panels; dark stage. Menu/lobby/nickname/co-op badge styles. **Neon chrome**: `ns-scanline` (animated strip along the bottom edge of the toolbar), `ns-flicker` (logo glow) and `ns-pulse` (breathing glow on the primary CTA), plus the `ns-neon-button` mixin that gives `.ns_btn`/`.ns_cta` a light sweep on hover. - `models/neon_strike_feedback.py` — `neon.strike.feedback`: bug reports and ideas sent from the public page (`kind`, `message`, `image` as `fields.Image`, `state` for triage, `note`). - **The public feedback endpoint is the only place the game accepts a file**: `/neon/feedback` caps the message (4000 chars) and the screenshot (3 MB decoded), throttles per session (15 s between reports, 20 per session) and, most importantly, **validates the bytes, not the declared mime type** (`IMAGE_MAGIC`) — an SVG, an HTML data URL or an executable renamed to `.png` are all rejected. If you add a format, add its magic bytes too.

Neither `lxml` nor `xmllint` is available in the sandbox, so a full RelaxNG pass is not possible — read the grammar and compare against a real core view of the same type before shipping an arch change.
- `views/neon_strike_views.xml` — `ir.actions.act_url` to `/neon` (the "Play" menu opens the page), a second one to `/neon?practice=1` (the **Practice** menu, see the practice-mode note below) + `ir.actions.act_window` for the leaderboard + menus. - `views/website_menu.xml` — `website.menu` record "Neon Strike" → `/neon` under `website.main_menu` (same pattern as `pingpong_3d/views/website_menu.xml`). This is what makes the game reachable from the public site navigation.

## Project conventions

- Standard header in every file: `Part of Odoo. See LICENSE file...` — keep it in new files.

- **Small-fry corpses ride the kill cue, like the drones'.** `boom.fr` is four small numbers — which of the four it was, its chassis, the wear it died wearing and the pose it died in — and everything else the break-up is drawn from is a pure function of those and the corpse's own age, so a guest that only ever receives the cue draws the same wreck. `dr` (drones) and `fr` (the rest) stay separate arrays on purpose: the drone path is byte-identical to what it was, and the two kits age on different clocks (`w.life`). The cap drops the **oldest** corpse rather than refusing the newest, which is the study's own note and was already what `_droneWreck` did.
- **A cue is for what a guest cannot observe, and nothing else.** HYDRA's whole death-and-rebuild of a side head is *observed*: `hd` carries one number per head — its hull points while it lives, minus the frames left of its death-and-rebuild once it does not — so a drop is a hit, zero is a destroyed head and the countdown under it is the regrow, and states 7/8/9 of the design sheet cost no bus traffic at all. The local hit flash in particular could never have been a cue: it fires as often as you can put a bullet on a head. What did need cues is the fan (`fanL`/`fanR`, one per mouth because the two fire a beat apart), because a fan lasts one frame and the snapshot a guest would have to spot it in arrives up to four frames later. VULCAN repeats both halves of the lesson: its shoulder fans carry the same one signed number each (`vf`), so the hit flash, the seized fan and the clearing are all free, while `vent`/`backfire`/`spit` are cues because each is one frame of a pattern leaving the hull. Its heat and phase (`ht`, `vp`) are neither — they are *levels*, they last as long as they last, and the whole hull is drawn from them.
- **Boss cosmetic cues travel as `bfx` on the `ev` channel**: `_bossCue(e, name, data)` fires the local effect and mirrors it to the guests, for the three things that cannot be observed from position alone (dreadnought burst, aimed salvo, hive launch) plus the prism shockwave. The alternative — deriving them on the guest from the AI's own arithmetic (`floor(e.t) % 85 === 0`) — would desync the animation the first time anyone retunes a boss. `_playEvent` creates the animator on demand, because the cue can arrive in the same snapshot that introduces the boss.

## Perks, dash and colossi (added in 19.0.5.0.0)

- **Perk phase**: after clearing a wave where `wave >= nextPerkWave` (steps of 5), `_openPerkPhase()` switches `state` to `"perk"`, rolls 3 offers **per ship** and freezes the field. Everyone picks (`pickPerk(slot, index)`); after `PERK_TIMEOUT` (20 s) the pending ones take the first card. Perks are per run: `reset()` wipes `ship.perks`.
- **Derived ship state**: `_recalcPerks(sp)` rebuilds `sp.mods` (numbers) and `sp.flags` (booleans) from `sp.perks`; nothing else may write them. Conditional perks are resolved live in `_fireDelay`, `_bulletDmg` and `_impactDmg`, never baked into the ship.
- **Dash (Space)**: free for everyone, no perk needed. `sp.dash > 0` means **intangible** — `hurtShip` and every collision check bail out. Six perks modify it (cooldown, charges, trail, ram, reflect, refund).
- **Actives (1-4)**: `sp.actives` is derived from the perks in pick order, capped at `MAX_ACTIVES`; `useActive(slot, i)` checks the cooldown and `_fireActive` does the work.
- **Remote input**: dash, actives and perk picks are **one-shot actions**. They travel in the `action` field of `/neon/input` (`dash`, `act<n>`, `perk<n>`) and the host applies them through `setRemoteAction`. The x/y in that payload is ignored, because the pointer queue keeps only the last position and would drop actions.
- **Camera**: `this.zoom` is a **render-only** concern (plus `_ptr`, which must invert the same `scale * zoom`). `_fitZoom` computes it **from the local canvas** so the playable field (and the whole boss hull, with a little air) exactly fills that screen — each client frames its own window, and nothing about the camera travels over the bus. It is recomputed on `resize()`. The HUD is drawn in its own unzoomed transform (`hudOx/hudOy`) so it never shrinks.
- **The playable field is dynamic**: `this.field` (read off the colossus, `COLOSSI[k].field`, 1.34-1.5) widens X by that factor and Y by `_fieldY` (60% of it, because screens are wide and that is what ends up filling them) widens the arena while one is alive, and `fx0/fx1/fy0/fy1` are the live bounds — **they can be negative**. Every "did it leave the field" check (ship clamp, bullets, enemy bullets, rocks bouncing, capsules, enemies falling out) and the AEGIS curtain / VULCAN rock barrage use those, never `0..W/H`. Both `zoom` and `field` snap to their target once within 0.002, otherwise the exponential easing leaves the walls fractionally off forever.
- **Wave pacing**: `spawnWave` **queues** the wave in `this.pending` and `_updateSpawns` releases it, bringing the next spawn forward whenever the field drops under `minAlive` (`3 + players + wave/5`, capped at 11). Do not go back to parking the whole wave above the top of the screen: the tail took ~12 s to fly in and the wave could not end until it did. Two more valves: stragglers get `e.rush` (1.9x speed) once the queue is empty and the wave is old, and a boss on its own gets a thin escort stream.
- **Run timer**: `engine.playMs` counts **wall clock**, not frames (`_tickClock`), so a 30 fps machine reports the same duration as a 60 fps one. It only advances while `playing` and not paused, which leaves out the pause screen and the perk phase, and single gaps are clamped to 100 ms so a backgrounded tab does not count as play time. It reaches the server through `onGameOver({seconds})` → `/neon/score` / `/neon/solo_score`.
- **Pause**: `Esc` (or the toolbar button) → `togglePause()` → the `pause` action, so a guest asks the host instead of freezing only itself. `_loopFn` returns right after the pause check, meaning **nothing** ticks while paused; the state travels as `pz` in the snapshot and `_setPaused` fires the `onPause` callback the OWL toolbar listens to.
- **All enemy damage goes through `_damageEnemy(e, dmg, killer)`**: it is the only place that applies WARDEN's armour (x0.35), routes a hit to a destructible part, applies VULCAN's vent multiplier and calls `killEnemy`. Never write `e.hp -=` anywhere else, or a new damage source will quietly ignore all four.
- **Boss variety**: `mkEnemy("boss")` reads `bossForWave` and copies the radius, tint, hull and payout from `BOSSES[k]`; the sprite comes from `ENEMY_SPRITES.boss` (generated from the same catalogue) via the usual `e.v` variant. `k` travels as `ck` in the snapshot and `armor` as `ar`, so the guest rebuilds the right hull and draws the shield ring.
- **Supply drops**: a boss fight kills the capsule flow (no small fry, no drops). `_updateSupply` drops one every ~7 s while `_bossPresent()`, and every boss sheds one per 25% of health lost (`e.dropAt`). Both use `dropPup(x, y, true)`, which rolls on the `supply` weights.
- **14 capsule types**: the original T/S/B/L plus R rapid, V overcharge, P piercing, H homing, D wingman, G phase, F freeze, X overload, C combo and Y payday. Adding one means four places: a `pup<X>` sprite, an entry in `PUP_COLORS`, a weight in **both** `PUP_TABLE` rows and a branch in `_applyPup` (+ `PUP_BUFFS`/`BUFF_KEYS` if it is timed). The timed ones live in `ship.buffs` (frames left) and are read **next to** `mods`/`flags`, which stay perks-only; they refresh instead of stacking and travel as the `bf` bitmask in the snapshot, so `BUFF_KEYS` is wire format — append only.
- **Colossus hull**: `d.hp + wave * 28`, halved-ish base so the wave term carries the growth — meeting the same colossus on a later cycle (they repeat every 50 waves) is a real step up. Tuned for roughly 15-28 s solo on the first encounter.
- **A colossus never uses the `flash` sprite**: it is hit every frame, so the white silhouette would leave it permanently washed out (and it would double the sprite cache for a ~850x260 canvas). Its hit feedback is the impact burst plus the top health bar.
- **Colossus hitbox is a box** (`hw`/`hh`), not a circle: use `_enemyHit(e, x, y, pad)` for bullets and ships. `e.r` stays as a rough circle for splashes, trails and the black hole. `_isBoss(e)` covers boss + colossus wherever the old `type === "boss"` check meant "big". **A colossus may also have destructible parts**, and HYDRA-07 does: `_enemyHit` falls through to `_headAt`, whose boxes come from `hullParts` (so what you can shoot is the cells that light up), and they reach below and outside the chest's box — half of each side head used to be unshootable. **Anything with a real point of impact passes it to `_damageEnemy(e, dmg, killer, hx, hy)`** so the hit can be routed to a part; area effects (a bomb, the EMP, a black hole, a trail) deliberately do not, and go to the hull. Head damage never comes off the hull. **VULCAN's shoulder fans are the other shape of this**: they sit *inside* the chest's box, so `_enemyHit` already covers them and only `_damageEnemy` has to know — it checks `_fanAt` before the hull, and the same position then decides whether the hit landed in the core box while the slot is open (`_ventOpen` + `_coreHit`, the one damage multiplier in the game). Fan damage never comes off the hull either, and a jammed fan stops being a target for the same reason a destroyed head does: letting bullets keep stopping there would quietly protect the chest behind it.
- **Beams** (`this.beams`) always telegraph first (`warn` frames of a dashed sight line) and only then damage. `src` anchors them to a hull so they follow it; they are removed when the owner dies (`killEnemy`) and on the perk phase.
- **Balance numbers live in the data files**, not the engine: perk `mods` in `perks.js`, colossus `hp`/`zoom`/`w` in `colossi.js`. Colossus hit points were tuned for a 25-45 s fight with a reasonable build (`hp + wave * 20 + 50% per extra player`).

## Bullet-hell layer (added in 19.0.11.0.0)

The game had every system a shmup needs except the ones that make one readable
and fair. This is that pass, and most of it is a contract between what the
player is shown and what the simulation does.

- **The hitbox is `SHIP_HIT_R` (6.5) and nothing else.** It used to be 16.5 for
  bullets, 13 for hulls, 12 for rocks and 8 for beams -- four different shapes,
  all of them at least as wide as the 32 px sprite, i.e. the opposite of the
  genre. Every one of them now goes through **`_hitR(sp)`**, so there is a
  single circle to learn, and `_drawHitbox` draws it. If you add a new threat,
  measure it against `_hitR`, never against a literal.
- **The dot is drawn even while the hull blinks.** `drawShip` used to `return`
  early during invulnerability; it now skips only the hull, so where you are is
  never ambiguous. The blink itself doubles in rhythm for the last ~25 frames
  and an arc drains around the ship: the old flat 4-on/4-off was identical on
  the first frame of the window and on the last, which is what made "I was hit
  right after my invulnerability" feel like the game cheating.
- **`_setInv(sp, frames)` is the only way to start an invulnerability window**,
  because it also records `invMax`, which is what the arc and the blink ramp
  read. Never write `sp.inv =` directly.
- **Focus (Shift)**: `sp.focus` multiplies the movement lerp by `FOCUS_FACTOR`
  and switches the hitbox to its explicit form. It is the one *held* input in
  the game: host/solo read `this.keys.shift` every frame in `update()`, and a
  guest -- whose channel only carries one-shot actions -- sends `focus1`/`focus0`
  edges, with `fc` mirrored in the snapshot so a lost edge is visible.
- **Grazing**: the enemy-bullet loop already walked every bullet against every
  ship, so the graze test is a second radius on the same distance. `b.gz` is a
  per-ship bitmask so a bullet counts once, and it is skipped while dashing or
  invulnerable (otherwise the safest move would also be the best-scoring one).
  Every `GRAZE_PER_COMBO` grazes is a combo step: that is the payment for
  flying into a pattern instead of camping the bottom of the arena.
- **`EB_KINDS` is the bullet vocabulary** (spread / aimed / lance / curtain).
  `_eb(x, y, vx, vy, k)` takes the kind at the point of fire and it travels as
  the 3rd slot of `eb` in the snapshot -- **wire format, append only**. Adding a
  pattern means choosing its kind; adding a kind means an entry here plus the
  line in the glossary note that teaches it.
- **`_every(e, key, period, mv)` replaced `Math.floor(e.t) % n === 0`.** The old
  form fired two or three frames in a row whenever `e.t` advanced by less than
  1 per frame, which is exactly what slow motion (0.35) and Time Warp (0.4) do:
  the radial burst tripled itself right after you were hit. Never go back to
  the modulo. `first` seeds the countdown (the old `% 55 === 27` offsets), and
  the boss base seeds `a1: 70` so nothing fires untelegraphed on arrival.
- **Telegraphs**: `_tel(e, left, kind)` turns the frames left on a pattern timer
  into `tel` (0..1) and `telK`, and `_drawTelegraph` draws it. Call `_tel`
  *before* `_every` consumes the timer. The strongest warning wins, so a boss
  running three timers still shows one cue. `tl`/`tk` travel in the snapshot:
  a guest does not simulate, and deriving the telegraph from the AI's own
  arithmetic would drift the first time anyone retunes a boss -- the same
  argument that already justifies `bfx`.
- **The curtain gap is decided one curtain ahead** (AEGIS and WARDEN), so the
  telegraph can point at it and `gp` can carry it. Where the hole is *is* the
  attack. The marker is deliberately narrower (52) than the real gap (62-66):
  what it points at is always safe. AEGIS has a second reader for it: the
  shutter `colossus_animator.js` opens on the hull above the hole, clamped to
  the hull edge when the gap falls past it (the hull is 828 px, the field 911).
- **Practice mode** is the test bench: *Neon Strike → Practice* in the backend
  opens `/neon?practice=1`, the glossary becomes a target picker (only on that
  query string — the public page never shows the buttons) and clicking a card
  starts a solo run whose every wave spawns **that target and nothing else**.
  The descriptor goes to the engine as the `practice` option and is read in
  `_spawnPracticeWave`; the wave counter keeps running, so the same target comes
  back a little tougher each time, but the **perk phase, the boss escort and the
  mixed spawn table are all skipped**, and `onGameOver` never posts the score.
  Asteroids are the one target that is not an enemy, so the wave-end check holds
  the wave open while any rock is still up. Restart keeps the target; Menu drops
  it and reopens the picker.
- **Boss phases are a health threshold, not a stopwatch.** `_bossRage(e, mv, at)`
  flips `raged` at `BOSS_RAGE_AT` / `COLOSSUS_RAGE_AT`, sets `hold` (a beat with
  no fire) and fires a `rage` event. It is deliberately **not** a `bfx` cue:
  `BossAnimator.emit` has no pose for it and the bus is the thing that makes
  co-op feel bad. Both health bars now draw a tick at the threshold.
- **Hitstop** (`this.hitstop`) freezes the whole simulation for a few frames on
  an impact, checked in `_loopFn` before the slow-motion clock. It never runs on
  a guest (it would only stutter the interpolation) and is skipped while
  `bombing`, or a bomb sweeping thirty hulls would stop the game thirty times.
- **Bombs are a stock**: `sp.bombs`, spent with X (`useBomb`), refilled by the B
  capsule. `bomb()` sets `this.bombing`, which `killEnemy` reads to pay half and
  skip the combo -- a bomb is a way out, not a scoring move.
- **Losing the combo to the clock has a cue now**: at x5 or more the timeout
  pops "COMBO LOST" at the local ship with a falling tone, and the HUD's combo
  block flickers through its last second (8 on / 8 off, the capsule idiom) —
  it was the one loss in the game with no feedback at all. Smaller combos
  still go silently, so the cue never nags. The graze step tone also climbs
  with the combo (`1100 + combo * 30` Hz), so a run of grazes is audibly
  going somewhere.
- **A bomb sweeps a shockwave ring** (`this.shocks`, `SHOCK_FRAMES`): cosmetic
  only — the clear is still instant — but the eased ring crossing the field is
  what makes thirty bullets vanishing read as a blast instead of a cut. It
  travels on the existing `bomb` event (now with `x`/`y`, additive) so a guest
  draws it from the detonation point.
- **Capsule flow is a rate, not a per-kill constant.** The drop off a fry kill
  is still one roll, but what the player feels is capsules a *minute*, and the
  two come apart badly across a run: a wave is 9-12 hulls in 6-8 s at wave 1 and
  32-44 in 14-17 by wave 16, so a flat 22% more than doubles the flow with the
  kill rate. Measured over three runs to wave 28 before the fix: 6-12 capsules a
  minute and 0.6-1.0 standing on the field over the first three waves, against
  24-40 a minute and 2.3-3.9 standing from wave 16 on -- enough that the 10 s
  timed buffs never lapsed and a late run stopped being about the build.
  `_dropFalloff()` eases the chance from 1 to `DROP_FALLOFF_MIN` by
  `DROP_FALLOFF_WAVE`, so the product stays put, and `DROP_FIELD_CAP` skips a
  roll while four capsules are already uncollected, which takes the spikes off.
  Lucky Charm **multiplies** rather than adding on top, so the perk is worth the
  same fraction of the flow at wave 30 as at wave 3. Measured after, six runs:
  14.7 / 16.0 / 17.1 / 13.3 / 10.9 capsules a minute across waves 1-5 / 6-10 /
  11-15 / 16-20 / 21-28, with 1.5 down to 0.9 standing on the field. Both apply
  to **fry only**: a supply drop and a boss bundle are the answer to a boss
  fight having no fry in it, and are deliberately left alone.
- **Risk pricing**: point-blank kills pay up to +50%, clearing a wave untouched
  pays a bonus in `spawnWave`, and being touched at all clears `waveClean` and
  the banked grazes. The `Y` capsule was multiplied by the combo outright (at
  wave 30 with x25 a single capsule paid more than a boss); it now scales with
  it. `C` went from +6 combo to +3 now that grazing feeds the same ladder.
- **Density has peaks and valleys again.** The spawn logic is built so pressure
  never drops *inside* a wave, which leaves the gaps *between* them as the only
  place to breathe: 26 frames -> 48, and 110 after a boss. `killEnemy` also
  clears `ebullets` when a boss dies -- dying to a wreck's leftovers during the
  celebration slow motion was the least fair death in the game. In exchange the
  in-wave ceiling was raised (`minAlive` 11 -> 14, drip floor 10 -> 7), which it
  had been hitting since wave 24.
- **Small fry scale in hull, not only in speed** (`_hp` in `mkEnemy`, one step
  per type). They used to keep the same 1-4 hp for a whole run while the player
  stacked damage perks every 5 waves, so past wave ~25 a wave was longer but
  never harder.
- **`deaths` is persisted** (`neon.strike.score.deaths`, through `/neon/score`
  and `/neon/solo_score`): the same score with two deaths and with twenty are
  not the same run. It also softens the next waves a little (`relief` in
  `spawnWave`), as a floor under the spiral where you die, lose the combo, and
  die again to the wave you were already struggling with.
- **The veil between the backdrop and the play field is per place** (`bgScrim(def)`,
  reading `p.veil`), and `BG_SCRIM` is only the fallback for the places not yet
  ported. Nine of the 27 paint in the same warm reds as enemy fire and scatter
  1-3 px motes the exact size of a bullet core, all in `lighter`: on the lava
  world or under a supernova a shot and the scenery were literally the same
  pixels. One flat 30% fixed those nine and taxed the other eighteen, which is
  the whole point of the pass -- DEEP SPACE now needs none at all and EVENT
  HORIZON needs 22%. `Backdrop` precomputes it as `bd.scrim`; the engine and the
  glossary stills both lay it down, because the card has to show the sky you fly
  in, not the unveiled art.

Balance knobs, if this needs retuning: `SHIP_HIT_R`, `GRAZE_R`,
`GRAZE_PER_COMBO`, `FOCUS_FACTOR`, `TELEGRAPH_FRAMES`, the `HITSTOP_*` set, the
`_hp` steps in `mkEnemy`, the `500 * wave` in the no-damage bonus and the
`DROP_CHANCE` / `DROP_FALLOFF_*` / `DROP_FIELD_CAP` set. For VULCAN
specifically: `VULCAN.heat` (the whole cycle is paced by the `1/210` beam rate),
`VULCAN.vent.coreMul` with `COLOSSI[2].hp` (they have to move together), and
`VULCAN_FAN.heatFull`/`repair`/`ventShare`, which are the exchange rate of the
fan lever. Retuning any of those means re-running the sweep behind the numbers
quoted above: the lever is a *safety* trade, not a damage one, and it is easy to
turn it back into a trap without noticing.

## Multiplayer, temporarily hidden

Remote co-op is **hidden, not removed**: the snapshot broadcast lags too much on
a real connection. `MULTIPLAYER_ENABLED = false` in `neon_strike_game.js` is the
only switch — it gates the UI (create match, join code, lobby, the room label in
the toolbar, the co-op wording) and the bus subscription. Controllers, models,
bus channels, `ir.websocket` and the host/guest engine roles are all untouched
and still tested by flipping the flag back to `true`. `pingpong_3d` carries the
same flag with the same meaning (there it also hides the `?net=loopback` bench).
When co-op comes back, the one thing that needs wiring is the **hull choice**:
today only the local player's hull reaches the engine, so on a host the other
ships fall back to the default hull. It has to travel through the lobby
(`neon.strike.participant`) next to the nickname.

## Known gotchas

- **The leaderboard column owns its overflow**, and it moved: it used to be the 300 px side panel (`.ns_leaderboard`), it is now `.ns_pause_scroll` inside the Esc overlay. Same rule, same reason — `min-height: 0` + `overflow-y: auto`, with `.ns_pause_cols` also at `min-height: 0`, because `.ns_glossary_panel` is capped at the viewport and a full board would otherwise grow the overlay until `.o_neon_strike_root` (fixed, `overflow: hidden`) clipped it. The row cap is also enforced client-side (`MAX_SCORES`, `topScores`), not only by the `/neon/scores` query.
- **`.ns_btn` is global, not the toolbar's**: it is used in the toolbar but also in "Join" (menu), "Leave" (lobby), "Close" (glossary) and every button in the Esc overlay. It must be defined at the `.o_neon_strike` level; if it is only nested inside `.ns_toolbar`, elsewhere it falls back to Bootstrap's `.btn` and comes out dark text on a dark panel (invisible button). The toolbar variant wins by specificity, not by order.
- **Sprites and multiplayer**: the chassis variant (`e.v` on enemies, `rk.v` on asteroids) is rolled when the entity is created and **travels in the snapshot**; otherwise host and guest would see different hulls. Same for `rt` (kamikaze rotation), `am` (sniper charge) and `fi` (the frames left of a tank's recoil), which the guest cannot recompute because it does not simulate — `fi` is on the wire for the three frames it lasts and no longer. A **kamikaze's throttle is deliberately not on the wire**: it is rebuilt from `tt` on both roles (`v0 + accel * t`, capped, all out of `FRY_ANIM.kami`), because the velocity it comes off is the one thing a guest never receives. Asteroids no longer carry the `spin` polygon in the snapshot: they are drawn with `rock0/rock1`.
- **Sprite orientation**: player ships face up and enemies face down, exactly as the grid is written. When rotating the kamikaze, `Math.PI/2` is subtracted because its hull points at +Y, not +X.
- The `AudioContext` requires a user gesture: it is initialised on `pointerdown` (the `audio()` method). Do not move it to `onMounted`.
- **The logical space is fixed per match, not fixed at 680×540**: the whole simulation uses `this.W/H`; the render scales to the canvas (`resize()` computes `scale/ox/oy`) and input maps physical→logical in `_ptr`. `_fitArena` shapes the arena like the window (`BASE_W`/`BASE_H` = the old 680×540 as the floor, aspect clamped to `MIN_ASPECT`..`MAX_ASPECT`) so a wide screen gets playable room instead of black bars: **only the long side grows**, the short one keeps its base size. Anything derived from the size is rebuilt by `_onArenaResized` (field bounds, star field, backdrop, ships clamped back inside). The host/guest invariant is still there and is what makes this safe: the arena travels in the snapshot as `aw`/`ah`, a **guest never sizes its own** (`resize()` skips `_fitArena` for it) and adopts the host's in `applySnapshot`. Enemy counts per wave were deliberately *not* scaled with the width: the extra room is dodging room, and scaling spawns would tilt a global leaderboard by screen size. The colossus zoom does **not** change the arena: the camera simply shows more world around it (the purple frame is the field boundary, now drawn on every frame).
- **Host-authoritative**: only the host simulates. The guest is pure render; its `update()` is `_guestUpdate` (interpolates ship positions, regenerates local particles/audio from `snapshot.ev`). Do not put game logic in the guest branch.
- **Bus cost**: the host broadcasts ~15 Hz and each guest sends input ~20 Hz via RPC (each send = one `bus.bus` row + a websocket push). Acceptable for 2–4 players in dev; this is not low-latency netcode.
- **`website=True` on `/neon` is mandatory**: `web.frontend_layout` is inherited by `portal.frontend_layout` and, in turn, by `website.layout`, which uses `website.id` unguarded. That `website` only enters the render context if `request.is_frontend` is True, and that is decided by `routing.get('website')` (`http_routing/models/ir_http.py`). With `website=False` the page fails with `KeyError: 'website'` on any DB that has `website` installed. The flag is harmless if `website` is not there.
- **Public without login**: there is no `orm`/`call_kw` on the client (that is `auth="user"`). Everything goes through JSON controllers `auth="public"` with `sudo()`. Identity is `request.session["neon_token"]` (uuid), NOT `res.users`.
- **Channel by capability**: the channel is `neon_strike_match_<access_token>` (the match uuid). `_build_bus_channel_list` allows it when a match with that token exists; knowing the token means you joined. Do not use the `id` in the channel (it is guessable).
- **Standalone mounting**: the game is mounted with `mount(...)` on `.o_neon_strike_root` in `whenReady` (it is not a client action). It needs `bus_service` and `rpc` to be in `web.assets_frontend`.
- `player_name`/`participant.name` are **computed stored** (nickname or `user_id.name`): do not write them directly.
- The leaderboard uses `t-out` (not `t-esc`, deprecated in OWL 2).
- Odoo 19 API used from memory (source not mounted): `bus.bus._sendone`, `bus_service.addChannel/subscribe/unsubscribe`, `ir.websocket._build_bus_channel_list`, `mountComponent`/`makeEnv`/`startServices`/`getTemplate`, `<t t-call-assets ... t-js/t-css>`. Verify signatures when testing against a real 19.0.
